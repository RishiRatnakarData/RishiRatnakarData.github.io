Consumer Finance Complaint Intelligence

An end-to-end analytics project using public Consumer Financial Protection Bureau complaint data to examine complaint volume, product and issue mix, response patterns, relief outcomes, and a leakage-aware classifier.

View GitHub RepositoryBack to Projects

Business questions

  • How did complaint volume change through 2025?
  • Which products and issues dominate the detail sample?
  • How do published response and relief classifications vary across companies?
  • Can information available near complaint intake identify complaints more likely to receive monetary or non-monetary relief?

Data design

The project separates two analytical purposes rather than treating one sample as if it represented every company total. The full-population monthly layer contains all 5,442,964 CFPB complaints recorded during 2025. A 5,000-record weekly-stratified detail sample provides manageable row-level data for exploratory breakdowns, SQL analysis, and modeling while preserving coverage across the year.

Selected findings

  • CFPB recorded 5,442,964 complaints during 2025.
  • Monthly volume increased from 335,236 in February to 519,786 in October, a 55.1% increase.
  • In the 5,000-row detail sample, credit reporting represented 4,379 complaints, or 87.6% of the sample.
  • The sampled relief rate was 40.6%, while 99.6% of sampled complaints had a timely response.
The sample-based company and issue figures are descriptive of the weekly-stratified detail sample; they are not presented as full-population market-share or quality rankings.

Modeling design

A logistic-regression model predicts whether a complaint receives monetary or non-monetary relief. Predictors are restricted to information available near complaint intake. Published company response, timely-response status, and relief outcomes are excluded from the feature set to avoid leakage. The evaluation uses an earlier-to-later time split rather than a random split.

Validation and reporting

  • Nine automated tests support the reproducible pipeline.
  • A three-page Power BI report presents the analytical outputs.
  • The repository documents the weekly-stratified sampling method, data-quality results, and interpretation limits.
What this project demonstrates: official API ingestion, analytical sampling design, SQL analysis, time-aware validation, leakage control, interpretable modeling, automated testing, and business-facing reporting.

Limitations

  • CFPB complaint records are not a statistical sample of all consumers or all financial activity.
  • Differences in complaint mix can affect company-level relief rates; the results should not be interpreted as causal quality rankings.
  • The classifier estimates predictive association with the published relief outcome, not the causal effect of a company action.
